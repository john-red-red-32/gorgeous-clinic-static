You have probably seen the NHS note that dermal filler complications can be serious, and that can feel alarming. That does not mean filler is always unsafe, but it does mean you should go in informed. This piece explains the main risks the NHS highlights, in plain terms: infection, lumps, blood vessel problems, nerve injury and rare but serious outcomes. It also explains what makes treatment safer: an experienced practitioner, the right product, a thorough consultation and sensible aftercare.

Understanding NHS Warnings: Dermal Filler Risks, What NHS advice means

When the NHS warns that dermal filler complications can be serious, it is reminding people that injectable treatments are medical procedures, not cosmetic shop purchases. The advice emphasises that while many people have trouble-free treatments, complications do occur and some need immediate clinical attention. This is why regulated training, sterile technique and clear aftercare are important.
A sensible takeaway is simple: ask questions, check training and consent, and treat warning notices as a prompt to choose care carefully rather than a reason to panic. A good consultation will explain both likely benefits and the small but real risks of treatment.

Common complications to know

Most complications fall into a few recognisable groups. Infection can arise if sterility is broken; it usually shows as increasing redness, swelling or pain and may need antibiotics. Small lumps or unevenness are common early on and often settle with massage, time or, for hyaluronic acid filler, an enzyme called hyaluronidase to dissolve product if needed. Vascular problems occur when filler is injected into or compresses a blood vessel, reducing blood flow. Prompt recognition and treatment are essential to reduce tissue damage. Rarely, misplaced filler can affect nerves, causing numbness or altered sensation. In very rare cases, product entering an artery can lead to vision loss, which is why facial anatomy knowledge and emergency plans matter.
Knowing these categories helps you spot when to seek urgent review. If redness and pain worsen, sudden vision changes occur or large areas feel numb, contact your clinic or local urgent care promptly.

Why practitioner training matters

Good outcomes are about more than a steady hand. Safe practice requires anatomy knowledge, infection-control habits, the ability to manage complications and access to appropriate emergency medicines such as hyaluronidase. Practitioners who understand facial vascular anatomy are less likely to cause vascular occlusion, and those trained in complication management are more likely to spot problems quickly and treat them effectively. Training that includes supervised practical work and ongoing CPD is a meaningful safeguard.
Ask about a clinician's clinical qualifications, where they trained and how often they update skills. Product choice and placement

Not all fillers are the same. Materials, particle size and manufacturer quality affect how a product behaves under the skin. Hyaluronic acid fillers are reversible with hyaluronidase, which is one reason many clinicians prefer them for common facial areas. Other products may give longer-lasting volume but are harder to correct if something goes wrong. Poor product choice or placing filler too superficially or too deeply are common causes of visible lumps, contour problems and prolonged swelling.
A careful clinician will choose a product for the specific facial area, explain why it is appropriate, and have a clear plan if the result is not as expected. If a practitioner cannot explain what they are using and why, consider that a red flag.

Aftercare, choosing a clinic and next steps

Aftercare and clear communication reduce risk. Clinics should give written aftercare instructions and a direct way to contact them if something feels wrong. Typical advice includes avoiding strenuous exercise, alcohol and facial massages for a short period, and watching for worsening redness, pain or altered vision. Clinics that offer a follow-up review within the first week show good safety habits.
When choosing where to go, check clinician qualifications, read recent reviews and favour medically trained teams who keep emergency medicines on site. For locals around Blackpool and the Fylde Coast, ask whether the clinic follows recognised guidance and can demonstrate complication management. If you are considering treatment, a consultation is the safest place to start.
